**An Analysis of the Diffusion of Power in Three Dimensions**

English translation of the Master's thesis by **Barbara I. S. Calderon**, originally published in Portuguese in **2018** at the **Federal University of Santa Catarina (UFSC), Brazil**.

### Abstract

> The diffusion of power was a phenomenon first described by Susan Strange
> in the context of International Political Economy (IPE) in 1996. The phenomenon
> encompasses the concepts of relational and structural power, which are essential
> for understanding the IPE discipline. Although described in the 1990s, the
> diffusion of power did not address the cyber domain, nor explicitly the technological
> revolutions that culminated in the global computer network. In cyberspace, various
> actors operate to impose their authority over individuals and services. In this
> context, at the beginning of the 21st century, the low-latency anonymous network
> known as "The Onion Router" emerged, being part of the so-called "Dark Web" — a
> region of cyberspace characterized by active efforts to shield communication,
> privacy, and specific users. This research aims, through journalistic articles
> published between 2007 and 2017, to analyze the diffusion of power in the TOR
> network across three dimensions: authority, control, and outcomes.

**Keywords:** Diffusion of Power · Dark Web · TOR Network · Structural Power · Relational Power · International Political Economy

### About the Research

This thesis extends **Susan Strange's diffusion of power framework** to include the concept of **dimensions of power**, analyzing how **non-state actors** shape global political economy through the **TOR network** across **authority, control, and outcomes**.
